Reflux and bloating

By the CalJab team, with sources · Last reviewed October 20, 2026

Heartburn, reflux, burping, gas and bloating are among the common side effects of GLP-1 medicines; they may affect up to 1 in 10 people. 1·2

For reflux

Reflux is stomach acid travelling up towards your throat; it causes a burning feeling in your chest and a sour taste in your mouth. 4 Symptoms are often worse after eating, when lying down and when bending over. 4

  • Eat smaller, more frequent meals. 4
  • Don't eat in the 3 or 4 hours before bed. 4
  • Don't wear clothes that are tight around your waist. 4
  • Cut down on food and drink that trigger your symptoms. Coffee, tomatoes, alcohol, chocolate and fatty or spicy food make reflux worse for some people. 4
  • Keep alcohol low. With GLP-1 treatment it can make nausea and reflux worse. 3·4
  • Raising the head of your bed by about 10 to 20 cm can help, but extra pillows can press on your belly and make it worse. 4
  • Don't smoke. 4

If you keep getting heartburn, your pharmacist can suggest an antacid; these are for the short term and should not be taken regularly for long periods. 4

For bloating

The most common reason for bloating is a lot of gas in your gut. 5 Some vegetables, fizzy drinks or swallowing air while you eat can cause it. 5

  • Chew with your mouth closed to avoid swallowing air. 5
  • Eat smaller, more frequent meals instead of large ones. 5
  • Drink plenty of water; go easy on fizzy drinks, alcohol and caffeine. 5
  • Don't eat lots of foods that cause gas, such as cabbage, beans or lentils. 5
  • Exercise regularly. It can help when you feel bloated too. 5
  • If you are constipated, eat foods high in soluble fiber such as oats or linseed. 5

When to see a doctor

  • Severe stomach pain that does not go away and may spread to your back: see a doctor straight away. It can be a sign of an inflamed pancreas. 1·2
  • Heartburn most days, lifestyle changes and pharmacy medicines not helping, food getting stuck in your throat or being sick often: see your doctor. 4
  • Bloating with vomiting, diarrhea or constipation, stomach ache, fever, a swelling or lump in your tummy, or not being able to pass gas or poo: contact a doctor right away. 5
  • Sudden severe stomach ache with a swollen stomach, vomiting blood or vomit that looks like coffee grounds, or severe trouble breathing: get emergency help. 5

Make decisions about your medicine and dose with your doctor.
