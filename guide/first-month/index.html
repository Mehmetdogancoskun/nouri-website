What to expect in the first month

By the CalJab team, with sources · Last reviewed October 21, 2026

GLP-1 medicines act on the systems that regulate appetite; you feel full sooner, less hungry and have fewer cravings. 1·2

The dose starts low

  • Treatment starts with a low dose and goes up step by step on your doctor's plan. 1·2
  • You stay on each dose for at least 4 weeks. 1·2
  • The dose goes up slowly to make digestive side effects less likely. 1
  • If nausea or vomiting is really bothering you, talk to your doctor about delaying the next increase or going back to the previous dose. 1
  • Don't change your dose unless your doctor tells you to. 2

What can happen in the first weeks

  • Nausea, diarrhea, vomiting and constipation are most common while the dose is going up and usually ease over time. 1·2
  • Going off some foods can happen too, mostly at the start and with dose increases. 3
  • With less appetite, hours can pass without eating. That can mean not getting enough nutrients, or reaching for sugary food later. 3
  • If your calorie intake drops very low, the risk of missing vitamins and minerals goes up. 3

Your injection day

  • Once a week, on the same day if you can; any time of day, with or without food. 1·2
  • If you need to change the day, at least 3 days should have passed since your last injection. 1·2
  • Thigh, tummy or upper arm. Change the spot each week; you can use the same area but not the exact same spot. 2·4
  • If you inject into your tummy, the Mounjaro leaflet says to stay at least 5 cm from your belly button. 2
  • If you miss a dose, what to do depends on the medicine; don't take two doses together to make up for it. 1·4
