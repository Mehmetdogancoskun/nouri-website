The three steps of FODMAP

By the CalJab team, with sources · Last reviewed October 21, 2026

FODMAPs are a group of sugars shown to irritate the gut and cause IBS symptoms such as bloating, gas, constipation, diarrhea and pain. 1 The Monash University Low FODMAP Diet has three steps, and the aim is a personal diet that is as little restrictive as possible for the long term. 1

1. Elimination (2 to 6 weeks)

  • You swap high FODMAP foods for low FODMAP alternatives; the aim is to get symptoms under control. 1
  • It is a swap diet, not a cut-out diet: for example, your daily apple for an orange, or onion for chives. 2
  • This step is best started under the supervision of a dietitian. 2

2. Reintroduction (8 to 12 weeks)

  • If your symptoms improved in step 1, it is time to bring FODMAPs back; you stay on a low FODMAP diet meanwhile. 1
  • Each FODMAP group is tested on its own: fructose, lactose, sorbitol, mannitol, fructans and GOS. You eat 1 test food once a day for 3 days. 1
  • Leave a break of a few days between tests; a dietitian helps you read your responses. 2

3. Personalization (long term)

  • FODMAPs you tolerate well go back into your diet; only those that trigger symptoms stay limited, and only enough to keep symptoms in check. 1
  • FODMAP tolerance can change over time; try foods that did not suit you again in a few months. 2

Before you start

The NHS advice is for people who have been diagnosed with IBS. 3 If you need to avoid lots of different foods to control your symptoms, see your doctor; they may refer you to a dietitian. 3 Not skipping meals and not eating too quickly are advised too. 3
