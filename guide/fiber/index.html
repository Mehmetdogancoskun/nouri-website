Getting enough fiber

By the CalJab team, with sources · Last reviewed October 21, 2026

Fiber helps your digestion and helps prevent constipation. 1 Eating plenty of it is also linked to a lower risk of heart disease, stroke, type 2 diabetes and bowel cancer. 1

How much

UK guidelines say adults should aim for 30 g of fiber a day; most adults eat about 20 g. 1 It's best to get it from a variety of foods. 1

Go gradually

  • In the first few days of a GLP-1 medicine, avoiding fatty or high-fiber foods can help with stomach side effects. 2
  • After that, increase fiber from foods gradually, and drink enough fluids alongside it. 2
  • If constipation continues despite changes to what you eat, fiber supplements may also help. 2

Where it comes from

  • Breakfast: porridge oats or a plain wholewheat cereal. 1
  • Bread, pasta and grains: wholemeal bread, wholewheat pasta, bulgur wheat or brown rice. 1
  • Potatoes with their skins on, such as a baked potato. 1
  • Pulses: add beans, lentils or chickpeas to stews, curries and salads. 1
  • Vegetables with meals, as a side or in sauces and stews. 1
  • Snacks: fresh fruit, vegetable sticks, oatcakes, or unsalted nuts and seeds. 1

Wholemeal spaghetti with a lentil and tomato sauce gives around 11 g of fiber. 1 On a food label, a high-fiber food has 6 g or more of fiber per 100 g. 1

If you use FODMAP mode

Many high FODMAP foods are also high in fiber, so getting enough can be harder on a low FODMAP diet. 3 Low FODMAP servings that add fiber: two thirds of a cup of firm tofu (6 g), a cup of cooked brown rice or quinoa (3 g), a medium orange (3 g), half an unpeeled potato (2 g) or a quarter cup of drained canned chickpeas (2 g). 3

In CalJab

Your fiber target and today's total are on Today. If you take a fiber supplement, add it in Health under Medicines; it's among the often added ones, and supplement reminders are in Reminders.

When to see a doctor

  • Sudden changes in your bowel habits, blood in your poo, needing to go at night, or severe or ongoing diarrhea: talk to your doctor. 3

If you have a bowel condition, ask your doctor or dietitian how much fiber suits you.
